  ## 臥龍会 三代目・日賀野 琢のコメント（見解）

  ここからは、臥龍会 三代目・日賀野 琢の受け止めです。

  日賀野は、栃木県書道連盟の会報で、令和四年から毎年、この登録運動の歩みを書き継いできました。以下は、その会報コラムからの抜粋です（いずれも本人の言葉）。

  > 令和四年（2022）　二〇一五年から活動する「日本書道ユネスコ登録推進協議会」も、今回の慶事に「国の法的担保を得られたことは大きな前進」とコメントした。いよいよ関係者が胸に着けた朱色の「わ」バッジが燦然と輝く日も近いのではと期待する。

  > 令和五年（2023）　「日本書道文化協会」を中心に、活発な動きを呈している。先に登録された「伝統的酒造り」に先を越されたものの、次こそは「書道」が確実という機運が盛り上がっている。約十年にわたる活動が結実する日も目前と言えよう。

  > 令和六年（2024）　本年一月、省庁連絡会議への提案が正式決定され、三月にユネスコ本部へ提案される。最終的に審議・決定されるのは二〇二六（令和八）年の十一月頃になる見込みである。あわせて、井茂圭洞先生の文化勲章、黒田賢一先生の文化功労者と、大きな慶事も相次いだ。

  > 令和七年（2025）　ユネスコ登録は日本の書道界全体の悲願である。だが、この慶事がいわゆる「一時的」なブームとして終わることの無いよう、斯界の末端に居るものとして、心して普及・伝承してゆかねば意味がない。これを肝に銘じる次第。

  > 令和八年（2026）　登録運動はいよいよ大詰め。十一月下旬、中国・福建省厦門（アモイ）で開かれる政府間委員会で正式発表の予定だ。この慶事が「一時的」なブームとして終わることのないよう、これからが肝心。普及・伝承してゆくべく、書道人の一致協力が大切であろう。

  五年を貫くのは、この慶事を一度きりの祝い事で終わらせないという思いです。今回の登録勧告にあたり、日賀野はあらためて次の三点を挙げます。

  1. 単なる慶事に終わらせず、継続してゆくこと。
  2. 中央の華やかな書壇だけでなく、地方の小さな書道教室までも大切にすること。
  3. 地道な普及活動を、これからも重ねていくこと。

  「書道が世界に認められることは、この上ない喜びです。けれど本当に大切なのは、その先。中央の華やかな舞台だけでなく、地方の小さな教室で筆を持つ一人ひとりまで、書の楽しさを伝え、次の世代へ受け継いでいくこと。地道な普及こそが、文化を生かし続けます」と日賀野は話します。

  ## 臥龍会のこれから

  臥龍会は一九四〇年の創立以来、月刊競書誌「臥龍」の発行と、全国の支部・教室での地道な活動を続けてきました。ユネスコ登録という大きな節目を、書の未来へつなぐ一歩として受けとめ、これからも墨とともに、普及と伝承を重ねてまいります。

  ## The news (reported facts)

  The following is based on news reports.

  - On 5 November 2026, the Agency for Cultural Affairs announced that a UNESCO advisory body had recommended Shodo for inscription as Intangible Cultural Heritage. At this stage it is "set to be inscribed"; the formal decision will be made by the intergovernmental committee.
  - That committee is held from 30 November to 5 December in Xiamen, Fujian, China.
  - If inscribed, Shodo would be Japan's 24th Intangible Cultural Heritage, following "traditional sake brewing" in 2024.
  - For background: in 2015 calligraphers established a council to promote UNESCO registration. In 2021 Shodo was selected, together with traditional sake brewing, as one of the first entries on Japan's register of Intangible Cultural Properties, and the Japan Shodo Culture Association was founded as its custodial body. The government applied for registration in 2024 and reapplied in 2025.

  ## Comment from Taku Higano, third-generation head of Garyukai (opinion)

  What follows is the personal view of Taku Higano, third-generation head of Garyukai.

  Since 2022, in the newsletter of the Tochigi Prefectural Calligraphy Federation, Higano has written about this registration effort year after year. The following are excerpts from those columns (all in his own words).

  > 2022　The council promoting UNESCO registration, active since 2015, welcomed the news: securing the state's legal safeguards is a major step forward. The day cannot be far off, he wrote, when the vermilion "wa" badge worn by those involved shines brightly.

  > 2023　Led by the Japan Shodo Culture Association, the movement is gaining momentum. Though "traditional sake brewing" was inscribed first, there is growing confidence that Shodo will surely be next. The day when nearly a decade of effort bears fruit seems close at hand.

  > 2024　In January the proposal was formally decided, and in March it was submitted to UNESCO headquarters. The final deliberation and decision are expected around November 2026. Happy news also came in pairs: the Order of Culture for Keido Inomo and Person of Cultural Merit for Kenichi Kuroda.

  > 2025　UNESCO registration is the long-cherished wish of Japan's entire calligraphy world. Yet, so that this happy occasion does not end as a mere "temporary" boom, those of us at the grass roots must take to heart that spreading and handing on the art is what gives it meaning.

  > 2026　The effort has reached its final stage, with formal announcement expected at the intergovernmental committee held in late November in Xiamen, Fujian, China. May it not end as a "temporary" boom; what comes next is what matters, and calligraphers must work together to spread and hand down the art.

  What runs through all five years is one wish: that this moment should not end as a one-time celebration. On this recommendation for inscription, Higano again raises three points:

  1. Not to let it end as a mere celebration, but to carry it forward.
  2. To value not only the grand central calligraphy world, but the small local calligraphy classrooms as well.
  3. To keep up the steady, patient work of spreading the art.

  "For calligraphy to be recognised by the world is a joy beyond words," Higano says. "But what truly matters is what comes after. Not only the grand central stage, but every single person holding a brush in a small local classroom — conveying to them the joy of the brush, and handing it on to the next generation. It is steady, patient outreach that keeps a culture alive."

  ## The road ahead for Garyukai

  Since its founding in 1940, Garyukai has published the monthly calligraphy journal Garyu and carried on steady work through its chapters and classrooms across Japan. We receive this milestone of UNESCO inscription as a step toward the future of calligraphy, and will continue, alongside ink, the patient work of spreading and handing down the art.
