  ## 臥龍会 三代目・日賀野 琢の想い（見解）

  ここからは、臥龍会 三代目・日賀野 琢の受け止めです。長く、栃木県書道連盟の会報でこの登録運動の歩みを見つめてきた日賀野は、今回の勧告を受けて、次のように語っています。

  「ユネスコ登録は、日本の書道界全体の悲願でした。二〇一五年からの約十年にわたる活動が、ようやく実を結ぼうとしている。心から嬉しく思います。

  けれど、本当に大切なのは、その先です。先に登録された『和食』や『伝統的酒造り』と同じように、決定の直後はマスコミに多く取り上げられるでしょう。しかし、この慶事がいわゆる『一時的』なブームとして終わってしまっては、意味がありません。これからが肝心です。

  中央の華やかな書壇だけでなく、地方の小さな書道教室で筆を持つ一人ひとりまで。書の楽しさを伝え、次の世代へ受け継いでいくこと。斯界の末端に居る者として、心して、その普及と伝承に努めてゆかねばと肝に銘じています。

  単なる慶事に終わらせず、地道な普及活動を、これからも一歩ずつ。書道人が一致協力して歩んでいくことこそ、何よりも大切だと考えています。」

  ## 臥龍会のこれから

  臥龍会は一九四〇年の創立以来、月刊競書誌「臥龍」の発行と、全国の支部・教室での地道な活動を続けてきました。ユネスコ登録という大きな節目を、書の未来へつなぐ一歩として受けとめ、これからも墨とともに、普及と伝承を重ねてまいります。

  ## The news (reported facts)

  The following is based on news reports.

  - On 5 November 2026, the Agency for Cultural Affairs announced that a UNESCO advisory body had recommended Shodo for inscription as Intangible Cultural Heritage. At this stage it is "set to be inscribed"; the formal decision will be made by the intergovernmental committee.
  - That committee is held from 30 November to 5 December in Xiamen, Fujian, China.
  - If inscribed, Shodo would be Japan's 24th Intangible Cultural Heritage, following "traditional sake brewing" in 2024.
  - For background: in 2015 calligraphers established a council to promote UNESCO registration. In 2021 Shodo was selected, together with traditional sake brewing, as one of the first entries on Japan's register of Intangible Cultural Properties, and the Japan Shodo Culture Association was founded as its custodial body. The government applied for registration in 2024 and reapplied in 2025.

  ## The thoughts of Taku Higano, third-generation head of Garyukai (opinion)

  What follows is the personal view of Taku Higano, third-generation head of Garyukai. Having long followed this registration effort in the newsletter of the Tochigi Prefectural Calligraphy Federation, he responds to the recommendation as follows.

  "UNESCO registration has been the long-cherished wish of Japan's entire calligraphy world. The effort begun around 2015, nearly a decade in the making, is at last coming to fruition. I am truly glad.

  But what truly matters is what comes next. As with washoku and traditional sake brewing before it, the media will give this much attention right after the decision. Yet if this happy occasion ends as a mere 'temporary' boom, it will have meant nothing. What comes next is what counts.

  Not only the grand central calligraphy world, but every single person holding a brush in a small local classroom. To convey to them the joy of the brush, and to hand it on to the next generation. As one working at the grass roots of this field, I take it to heart that I must devote myself to spreading and passing it on.

  Rather than let it end as a mere celebration, we will carry on the steady, patient work of outreach, one step at a time. For calligraphers to walk forward together, in one accord — that, above all, is what matters."

  ## The road ahead for Garyukai

  Since its founding in 1940, Garyukai has published the monthly calligraphy journal Garyu and carried on steady work through its chapters and classrooms across Japan. We receive this milestone of UNESCO inscription as a step toward the future of calligraphy, and will continue, alongside ink, the patient work of spreading and handing down the art.
